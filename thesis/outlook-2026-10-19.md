# 6-12 month outlook — October 19, 2026

## The regime ahead
**The base case is late-cycle "stagflation-lite."**
- **Inflation:** Headline inflation is sticky because of energy. CPI is 3.7% y/y, PPI 6.3% and core PCE 3.0%.
- **Labor:** The labor market is close to stalling but is not cracking. Payrolls average +51k over 3 months, while claims are only 199k.
- **Growth:** Retail sales are +6.3% y/y and the Empire and Philly surveys are expanding.
- **The Fed:** It is still tightening. Futures put a December hike at 92% and imply about 4.6% by June 2027.
- **Survey views:** A CNBC survey now expects at least two more hikes over the next year ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/cnbc-fed-survey-two-rate-hikes-seen-ahead.html)). New York Fed President Williams sees one more and then a pause ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/john-williams-fed-rate-hikes-2027.html)).

**What that means for markets:**
- **Valuations:** The 10Y is 5.1%, at the 98th percentile of 10 years, and the 10Y real yield is 2.8%. Market cap to GDP is 258%. Equity risk premiums are negative: SPY -1.0pp, QQQ -1.5pp.
- **What wins:** Earnings that do not depend on falling rates or cheap AI financing. That means energy cash flows, health care, insurance float income, defense, and defensive retail.
- **Oil:** WTI is near $99 and Brent about $110, with a live Hormuz risk premium. Its direction is the biggest swing factor.

**The two other paths:**
- **Soft landing (lower probability):** Oil below $80, core cooling and the Fed done in December. This favours the growth and semis names we are underweight.
- **Hard landing:** Claims above 250k, credit stress in AI infrastructure spreading (HY spreads are already up from 2.63 to 3.31pp since August), and the Fed overtightening.

## Themes
**Energy supply squeeze (SLB, WMB).**
- **Oil:** It is +72% y/y, and inventories drew sharply in two straight weeks.
- **Why services and midstream:** Rather than chase XOM and COP, both at highs with RSI above 70 and above their analyst targets, the portfolio uses names that have lagged crude.
- **SLB:** -18.6% from its high and basing above a 4-touch support at 47.5. Analyst target 62.4.
- **WMB:** A fee-based gas pipeline that benefits from LNG and power-generation demand. It sits at a 6-touch support near 68.4 (analyst target 85).
- **Entries:** Both are zone entries at support.

**Quality health care (LLY, JNJ).** This is the strongest sector over 6 months (+11.6pp vs SPY), and its earnings do not depend on the cycle.
- **LLY:** A 7/7 Stage 2 leader. The entry waits for a pullback toward the 50-day (about 1181) rather than buying 3% below the high. Q3 results are expected around Oct 29 (date unconfirmed, [forex.com](https://www.forex.com/en/earnings-release/)).
- **JNJ:** Down 9.5% from its high to stacked supports at 249.8 and 243.2. It is a lower-beta anchor.

**Higher-for-longer financials (PGR).** With a 13-week bill above 4% and the Fed still hiking, P&C insurers earn more on float. Auto pricing has kept pace with claims inflation. PGR trades at a forward P/E of 13.4 on a 6-touch support near 216.5. Banks are avoided: regional banks lag, and credit spreads are widening.

**Defense and geopolitics (LMT).** Houthi attacks, US security alerts across the Middle East and munitions replenishment support demand ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/19/live-updates-bitcoin-tags-usd83-000-and-slips-back-as-chip-stocks-lead-a-rebound)).
- **Why LMT:** It is basing above a 5-touch support at 548.9 on a forward P/E of 16.9, with an analyst target of 619.
- **Why not RTX:** It is in Stage 4 and was left out.

**Defensive consumer compounder (COST).** Consumer sentiment is at the 5th percentile, and housing and rates squeeze households. Warehouse membership retail gains share in that setting. COST sits on an 11-touch support near 996. Its forward P/E of about 40 is the main risk, so it is conviction 1. WMT was considered but is extended (RSI 74) with weaker structure (3/7).

**Power and grid build-out (GEV).** Power demand from data centres and grid replacement is a multi-year backlog story that should outlast a slowdown in AI financing. GEV is 24% off its high at a 6-touch support near 877 (analyst target 1224). It is conviction 1 because it carries AI-sentiment beta: Meta's capex update on Wednesday is a near-term test.

## Risks to the view
- **Oil reversal:** A durable Iran/Hormuz settlement would take oil below $80. Energy would lose its leadership and long-duration growth would rebound.
- **Fed overtightening into a contraction:** Claims above 250k or negative payroll prints would hit cyclicals (GEV, SLB) and insurers' equity portfolios.
- **Broad de-rating:** With market cap/GDP at 258%, a de-rating would not spare defensives entirely.
- **Policy:** Drug pricing (LLY, JNJ) or a government funding standoff (LMT).
