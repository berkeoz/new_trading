# 6-12 month outlook — November 12, 2026

## The regime ahead
The most likely regime over the next 6–12 months is **overheat**: above-trend growth, a labor market that has re-tightened and inflation that is sticky rather than falling.
- **Growth:** Q3 GDP grew 3.4% annualized and retail sales are up 6.3% y/y.
- **Labor:** October payrolls rose 165K with unemployment at 4.1%, and claims are 200K.
- **Inflation:** October core CPI rose 0.3% for the third straight month, a 3.8% annualized pace.
- **Fed:** fed funds futures price a December hike as certain (implied 4.17%) and about 4.7% by mid-2027.
- **Rates and dollar:** the 10Y is 5.07% with real yields at the 98th percentile, and the dollar is at a 3-month high.

In this regime, companies that earn more from higher rates and nominal growth should do best: banks, insurers, energy producers and defense. The same goes for businesses whose earnings do not depend on the cycle (quality health care, warehouse and discount retail).

Long-duration growth is the main loser. That means AI hardware, where valuations meet a negative equity risk premium, policy risk (export controls, data-center moratoria) and, since today, a leadership crisis at Nvidia.
