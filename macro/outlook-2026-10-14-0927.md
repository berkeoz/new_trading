# Macro outlook — October 14, 2026
**Big picture:** Inflation is re-accelerating, mostly from energy, but core is now also firming, while the labor market has nearly stalled. September CPI was hot: headline 3.7% y/y, with core at 0.3% m/m and 2.6% y/y, both above consensus. Futures in the data price an October 28 hike at 52% (up from 18% on Oct 10) and a December hike as certain, with the policy rate near 4.7% by mid-2027. That is a hostile mix for long-duration equities. It supports the dollar, keeps the 10Y at a 10-year high near 5.3%, and favours energy over rate-sensitive groups.

## What changed
- **The trigger: September CPI (released today, 08:30 ET):**
  - Headline rose 0.6% m/m (consensus 0.6%, prior 0.4%) and 3.7% y/y (consensus 3.6%, prior 3.4%).
  - Core rose 0.3% m/m (consensus 0.2%) and 2.6% y/y (consensus 2.5%, prior 2.4%) ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/fed-rate-hike-odds-surge-to-90-on-monthly-jump-in-core-prices-131206510.html)).
  - The energy index jumped 5.2% and gasoline 9.1%, about half of the headline gain. Food was flat ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/cpi-inflation-report-september-2026.html)).
  - The data feed shows headline at +0.65% m/m and core at +0.33%. Core's 3-month annualized pace is now 3.4% and headline's 4.6%.
- **Market reaction:**
  - Reuters says October hike odds rose to about 40% from 22% ([Investing.com](https://investing.com/news/economy-news/fed-rate-hike-seen-more-likely-after-inflation-data-4949259)); CoinDesk cited 44% ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/14/live-updates-bitcoin-tumbles-from-near-usd84-000-ahead-of-u-s-inflation-data)).
  - The futures in the data (52%) are higher still.
  - The 5Y rose 13bp to 5.14% and the 10Y 10bp to 5.32%, and DXY firmed to 102.8. Nasdaq futures fell about 0.7% and Russell futures about 1.2%.
- **Versus the previous outlook (Oct 10):**
  - That outlook's base case needed "core CPI at 0.2–0.3% m/m". This print is at the top edge of that range. Its claim that "core is drifting lower" no longer holds: core y/y has risen for the first time since May, and the 3-month pace has doubled from 2.0%.
  - The odds shift toward the downside (stagflation-scare) case, though its trigger of core at 0.4% or more was not hit.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:** Headline CPI is 3.72% (75th 10-year percentile), up from 2.4% in January and back near the 4.17% May peak. Core CPI is 2.57% (50th percentile), from 2.45% in August. Core PCE, the Fed's target, is 3.01% (65th). PPI is 5.4% y/y.
- **What it means:** Expectations are mixed. 5-year breakevens are anchored at 2.33%, but UMich 1-year expectations are 4.0% and Conference Board 12-month expectations are 6.1% ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/09/30/consumer-confidence-conference-board-september-2026)). The ISM manufacturing prices index jumped to 77.9 from 71.1, so pipeline pressure is building ([PR Newswire/ISM](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/manufacturing-pmi-at-54-5-september-2026-ism-manufacturing-pmi-report-302894520.html)). This is far below the 2022 peak near 9%, but the direction is now up.

**Labor:**
- **Data:**
  - Unemployment is 4.2% (55th percentile) and payrolls rose only +29K in September (3-month average +51K).
  - ADP was firmer at +90K (vs about 68K expected) ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/private-sector-jobs-rose-by-90000-in-september-better-than-expected-adp-reports.html)).
  - Claims are very low at 197K (2nd percentile), and continuing claims are 1.72M.
  - JOLTS openings are 7.08M (36th). Wage growth has slowed to 3.0% y/y (21st percentile).
- **What it means:** Low hiring and low firing. Wages are not the inflation source.

**Growth:**
- **Data:**
  - Q2 GDP rose 2.2% annualized, and retail sales are up 5.4% y/y.
  - ISM manufacturing is 54.5, its ninth month of expansion. Philly Fed is 37.8 (94th percentile).
  - Sentiment is very weak: UMich is 51.7 (5th percentile), and Conference Board confidence fell to 81.9, the lowest since 2014.
  - Housing starts are 1.28M (28th) and existing sales 3.99M (8th).
- **What it means:** Activity is solid, while consumers feel squeezed by prices.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** Fed funds is 4.00% (upper), giving a real rate of about +1.0pp. That is mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:** The 2Y is 4.80% (92nd percentile). The 10Y is 5.32% intraday (feed month value 5.24%, 98th percentile, the top of its 10-year range; about 1.9% in 2019, about 4.2% at the end of 2022). The 10Y TIPS real yield is 2.91% (98th).
- **Curve:** positive (10Y−2Y +0.42pp, 10Y−3M +0.95pp). The yield rise is now led by the 5Y, which means hike repricing.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Data:** The Chicago Fed index is -0.49 (loose), and the high-yield spread is 3.11pp (51st percentile). M2 is growing 5.7% y/y. The broad dollar is 121.6 (80th).
- **What it means:** Conditions remain easy despite record real yields, which argues for more Fed tightening.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** The Sahm rule reads 0.00, and the NY Fed's 12-month recession odds are 11.7%, falling. The Chicago Fed activity index (CFNAI, 3-month average) is +0.01. The Chauvet-Piger probability is 0.6%.
- **What it means:** Nothing is flashing.

**Valuation:**
- **Data:** The Buffett indicator is 262% of GDP, a 10-year high (97th percentile). The dot-com peak was 163% on the same measure.
- **What it means:** Equity risk premiums are negative (SPY -1.27pp, QQQ -1.80pp, SOXX -2.19pp vs the 10Y). That leaves little cushion for higher yields.

**Oil:**
- **Data:**
  - WTI spot is $96 (+56% y/y, 92nd percentile). EIA Brent spot is $125 (98th), and Brent futures are at $105.6. OVX is 48.5 (78th).
  - The latest EIA weekly report (week to Oct 2) showed a 3.2M-barrel draw vs a 1.7M build expected; stocks are 424.1M barrels ([Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/crude-inventories-10-2/)).
  - The IEA says OECD government stocks are at the lowest since 1989.
- **Risks:** Houthi strikes on Aramco and Hormuz incidents keep the supply risk premium high ([ZeroHedge](https://zerohedge.com/markets/futures-rise-ahead-hot-cpi-houthis-torch-aramco-hq-asml-beat-fails-revive-chip-trade)).

## Fed path
- **Futures in the data (effective rate 3.88%):**
  - Oct 28: hike 52%, hold 48% (implied 4.01%).
  - Dec 9: hike 100% (implied 4.26%).
  - The implied rate reaches 4.46% by March 2027 and 4.735% by June 2027, so roughly three more hikes are priced.
- **FedWatch/Reuters:** about 40–44% for October, after 22% before the print. The data feed runs hotter.
- **Fed speakers:**
  - Williams (voter, Oct 13): one more hike "may be appropriate", with no need seen for more, which is less than the market prices ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/john-williams-fed-rate-hikes-2027.html)).
  - Barkin (non-voter): persistent inflation warrants action.
  - Collins: has favoured another hike this year.
  - Still to come: Barkin (13:30 today), the Beige Book (14:00), Hammack (Thu 16:30) and Chair Warsh (Thu 23:30 ET).
- **What to watch:** If Warsh does not push back on October pricing, the hike odds likely firm.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:**
  - Higher-for-longer pressures multiples, mostly in rate-sensitive tech and semis (negative equity risk premiums, crowded positioning after a large 6-month lead).
  - Financials benefit from a positive curve but face mixed earnings (BAC miss, MS beat).
  - Energy remains the hedge against oil and inflation.
  - Small caps, homebuilders, real estate and regional banks are the most exposed to the 5Y jump.
- **Curve:** may flatten slightly as the front end reprices hikes.
- **Dollar:** firm on rate differentials.
- **Oil:** two-way, driven by headlines, with an upward bias while Saudi infrastructure is under attack.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (about 45%), sticky inflation and a slow labor market:**
  - The Fed hikes once more (October or December) and then holds near 4.25%.
  - The 10Y holds 5.1–5.5%. Equities chop sideways, with defensive and energy leadership.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI at 0.2–0.3% m/m, payrolls of 0–100K and WTI at $85–100.
- **Upside case (about 20%), soft landing:**
  - A Hormuz/Iran settlement takes WTI below $80, and core cools to 0.2% or less.
  - Hike pricing beyond December fades, and the 10Y falls below 5%.
  - Small caps and rate-sensitive groups catch up.
- **Downside case (about 35%, raised from 25%), stagflation scare:**
  - Core runs at 0.3–0.4% again, PPI and retail sales stay hot, and oil spikes on Saudi or Hormuz escalation.
  - The Fed hikes in October and December. The 10Y moves above 5.5%, high-yield spreads widen and valuations compress.
  - Or payrolls turn negative while inflation stays high.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI at 0.4% or more, Brent above $115, claims above 250K, or a Sahm reading of 0.3 or more.
