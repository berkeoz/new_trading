# Macro outlook — November 3, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is unchanged: strong growth, sticky and energy-led inflation, and a labor market that hires slowly but does not fire. This week's data leans hawkish:
- **JOLTS:** September openings rose to 7.52M, well above the ~7.10M consensus.
- **ISM:** October manufacturing was 55.5 (vs 54.5 expected), and prices paid were 75.2.

Futures price a December 9 hike at 82%, with the implied path at about 4.42% by mid-2027. For markets:
- **Equities:** supported by growth, but valuations are stretched and leadership is rotating.
- **Bonds:** the 10Y is back near 4.95%.
- **Dollar:** firm.
- **Oil:** $95–105, driven by Iran headlines.

## What changed
- **JOLTS (September):**
  - Openings were 7.52M vs about 7.10M expected and a revised 7.19M prior. August was revised up from 7.08M. This is the 62nd 10-year percentile.
  - The hiring rate was 3.4% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-november-3-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-midterms-amd-earnings-141143244.html)).
  - Quits and layoffs were expected at 3.09M and 1.66M. The actuals are n/a in the sources found.
  - Reports put hires near 5.4M and layoffs near 1.6M ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/cards/xpost-01m6gywdtkdn919dm0m5zp8eb0)).
- **What it means:** Demand for labour is firming again after a summer slowdown. This contradicts the weak payroll trend (3-month average +51K).
- **ISM manufacturing (October, released Nov 2):**
  - The headline was 55.5 vs 54.5 expected and 54.5 prior: a tenth month of expansion.
  - Production reached 59.0, the highest since November 2021, and employment was 52.4.
  - Prices paid eased to 75.2 from 77.9 but are still above 70 for a ninth month ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/us-ism-manufacturing-pmi-for-october-55-5-versus-54-5-estimate/), [TD Economics](https://economics.td.com/us-ism-manufacturing-index)).
- **Market reaction:**
  - Yields rose across the curve: 13-week bill +4.3bp, 5Y +5.0bp, 10Y +5.8bp to 4.952%, 30Y +5.5bp.
  - Futures held the December hike at 82%.
  - Stocks were mixed: semis +2.2%, while homebuilders (-3.1%) and staples (-2.0%) fell on higher yields.
- **Versus the Oct 29 outlook:** The base case is confirmed, and the hawkish tail is slightly fatter.
  - The labour market is not cooling as fast as payrolls suggested.
  - Input-price pressure in manufacturing is still extreme.
  - The 10Y has not returned above 5%, so the stagflation-plus-valuation downside is unchanged rather than larger.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:** headline CPI 3.72% y/y (75th 10-year percentile; 3-month annualized 4.6%); core CPI 2.57% (50th); PCE 3.65% (78th); core PCE 3.12% (69th; 3-month annualized 2.9%). PPI is 6.35% (82nd; 3-month annualized 8.1%), and ISM prices paid is 75.2, so pipeline pressure is still high.
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens 2.39% (66th), down from 2.46%; UMich 1-year expectations 4.6%.
- **Trend:** headline is up from 2.4% in January. That is above 2019's ~1.5–2% and below the 2022 peak near 9% CPI. Energy drives it; core is steady.

**Labor:**
- **Payrolls:** +29K in September (3-month average +51K, 12th percentile). Unemployment is 4.2% (Sahm 0.00).
- **Claims:** 194K, near a 10-year low (2nd percentile); continuing claims 1.70M (22nd).
- **Openings and wages:** JOLTS 7.52M (62nd), up from 7.19M. Wages are 3.0% y/y (21st, falling; 3-month annualized 2.4%).
- **ADP:** the latest print is September at +90K ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/private-sector-jobs-rose-by-90000-in-september-better-than-expected-adp-reports.html)). October is due Wednesday.
- **What it means:** low firing, slow hiring and soft wages. Labour is not the inflation source, but it is no reason for the Fed to ease either.

**Growth:**
- **Data:** Q3 GDP 3.4% annualized, retail sales +6.3% y/y (79th), industrial production +2.2%, durable goods +10.0% y/y.
- **Surveys:** ISM manufacturing 55.5; Empire 25.5 (92nd) and Philly 20.9 expanding. ISM services was 54.9 in September ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/ism-non-manufacturing-pmi-54-9-versus-55-2-estimate/)), and October is due Wednesday.
- **Housing:** weak. Starts 1.22M (15th), existing sales 3.99M (8th).
- **Sentiment:** split. UMich 48.1 (1st percentile) vs Conference Board 91.7 in October ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-consumer-confidence-increased-in-october-302920731.html)). The Conference Board leading index is n/a.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** fed funds 4.00% (upper) and core PCE 3.1%, a real rate of about +0.9pp. That is mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:** 2Y 4.56% (Oct 30; 88th). 10Y 4.952% live (98th-percentile area). 10Y TIPS 2.60% (98th).
- **Curve:** 10Y−2Y +0.32pp and 10Y−3M +0.70pp, both normal and slightly flatter than last week.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Data:** the Chicago Fed index is -0.49, loose. The HY spread is 3.43pp (84th), up from 2.63 in August, and the high-yield ETF is near its 3-month low. M2 is +5.7% y/y. The Fed's balance sheet is $6.75T.
- **What it means:** conditions are loose overall, but credit has been softening for two months. That is the first crack to watch.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00, NY Fed 12-month odds 16.4% (up from 13.4%), CFNAI 3-month average +0.03, Chauvet-Piger 0.4%.
- **What it means:** none is flashing.

**Valuation:**
- **Buffett indicator:** 255% of GDP (95th percentile), far above the 163% dot-com peak on the same measure.
- **Risk premium:** earnings yields vs the 10Y are negative: SPY -0.78pp, QQQ -1.14pp, SOXX -1.31pp.
- **What it means:** misses are punished (Alphabet, Shopify, Apple's margin guide).

**Oil:**
- **Prices:** WTI futures $96.8 and spot $103.4 (Oct 27; +67% y/y, 96th percentile). Brent futures $104.5; EIA spot $123. OVX 62.7 (92nd).
- **Inventories:** the latest EIA report (week to Oct 23) showed a 1.9M-barrel crude draw ([Azernews](https://www.azernews.az/oil_and_gas/266449.html)).
- **Drivers:** OPEC+ kept targets unchanged through December. Brent fell ~8% over 5 days on US–Iran deal hopes despite the Qeshm strikes.

## Fed path
- **Futures (effective rate 3.88%):** Dec 9 implied 4.085%, a hike priced at 82% and a hold at 18%. The implied average is 4.21% in March and 4.42% by June 2027, about two more hikes after December.
- **FedWatch:** reports after the FOMC put December hike odds at ~83–85% ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/federal-reserve-interest-rate-decision-october-28-2026), [Norada](https://www.noradarealestate.com/blog/interest-rates-hike-odds-december-2026-jump-85-after-feds-recent-decision/)). That is consistent with the futures. No current FedWatch readout was found.
- **Signals:** Hammack and Cook dissented for a hike in October, and two Fed presidents said last week that rates must rise. No Fed speakers spoke today.
- **Upcoming:** Hammack and Barkin (Thu) and Logan, Williams, Musalem and Bowman (Fri) come after payrolls.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** strong nominal growth supports earnings, but negative risk premiums leave no room for misses. Expect choppy indexes with rotation.
- **Tech and semis:** most exposed to the 10Y and AI-capex doubts, but deeply oversold (SOX ~22% off its high).
- **Health care and staples:** have led on a defensive bid.
- **Rate-sensitive groups:** homebuilders, regional banks and small caps trade the 10Y around 5%.
- **Financials:** benefit from a steeper front end.
- **Energy:** keeps support from $95–105 oil.
- **Curve, dollar and oil:** a modestly upward-sloping curve; a dollar firm on US outperformance (DXY ~102); oil headline-driven.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (~55%), sticky inflation with one or two more hikes:**
  - December hike to 4.00–4.25%, then a pause. The 10Y is 4.8–5.3%; equities range-bound with rotating leadership.
  - *Confirmed by:* payrolls 0–100K, core 0.2–0.3% m/m, ISM services above 53, HY below 3.5pp.
- **Upside case (~15%), soft landing:**
  - An Iran deal reopens Hormuz, WTI falls below $85, core cools and the December hike is priced out; the 10Y drops below 4.6%.
  - *Confirmed by:* a signed deal, falling breakevens, small caps and transports leading.
- **Downside case (~30%), stagflation plus a valuation or credit scare:**
  - Brent goes above $125; hikes run into 2027; AI-capex funding strain spreads.
  - *Confirmed by:* core at 0.4%+, HY above 3.75pp, the 10Y above 5.3%, claims above 250K.
