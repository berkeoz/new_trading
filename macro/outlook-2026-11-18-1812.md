# Macro outlook — November 18, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is still **overheat**: solid growth (Q3 GDP 3.4%), a labor market near full employment (unemployment 4.1%) and sticky inflation (core PCE 3.1%).
- **FOMC minutes:** the October minutes confirm the committee's bias is to keep hiking. "Almost all participants" judged further increases likely appropriate, and a couple raised more than 25bp.
- **Fed pricing:** futures price a certain Dec 9 hike (implied 4.175%) and about 4.6% by mid-2027.
- **Markets:**
  - Stocks are range-bound to lower while the 10Y sits near 5.1% and equity risk premiums are negative.
  - The dollar is at a 3-month high.
  - Oil has broken to 3-month lows (WTI 82) despite the Gulf flare-up. That is the main source of disinflation hope for 2027.

## What changed
**FOMC minutes, Oct 27–28 meeting (released Nov 18, 14:00 ET)** ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm)):
- **The vote:** the hold at 3.75–4.00% was 10–2; Cook and Hammack wanted 25bp. FXStreet called it "a tight decision" ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/fed-minutes-set-to-provide-further-clues-on-the-central-banks-hiking-pace-202611181500)).
- **Further hikes:** "almost all participants" saw further increases as likely appropriate under their baseline. "A couple" said a move larger than 25bp could warrant consideration if inflation data were unfavorable. Several preferred to judge the overall path rather than single meetings.
- **Inflation:** the vast majority saw upside risks. Staff expect inflation to step down sharply next year and reach about 2% in 2028, with risks skewed up, including the risk that longer-run expectations become unanchored.
- **Growth:** expanding at a strong pace, with AI investment strong and housing and other rate-sensitive sectors weak.
- **Financial stability:**
  - Almost all participants discussed the risk that AI returns disappoint; most noted fast-growing AI-related debt in "complex, opaque structures".
  - Staff flagged hedge-fund leverage.
- **Balance sheet:** reserve-management purchases stay at zero.

**Also today:**
- **Housing:**
  - October housing starts +9.9% to 1.309M, led by multifamily; permits -7.4% to 1.295M (12th 10-year percentile).
  - Pending home sales -4.7% to a record-low index of 63 ([Capp Notes](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-wednesday-november-18)).
- **Waller:** told CNBC a 50bp December hike remains possible.
- **Market reaction:**
  - The 2Y was unchanged at 4.80%; the 10Y fell 3.7bp to 5.066% as oil (-4.7%), gold (-3.5%) and copper (-5.2%) slid.
  - Kalshi priced 91% for 25bp in December and 8% for more. Yahoo Finance cites FedWatch at 69% for 50bp of cumulative hikes by end-January, up from 38% a month ago ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-november-18-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-nvidia-earnings-anthropic-ipo-143530588.html)).

**Versus the Nov 17 outlook:**
- **Base case:** unchanged and slightly firmer. The minutes validate the "two or three more hikes" path.
- **Larger hike:** a 50bp December move is back on the table rhetorically, but market pricing still favours 25bp.
- **Oil:** the new element. WTI's break to 82 lowers the odds of the stagflation leg of the downside case.
- **Downside case:** the risk of an AI de-rating now carries the Fed's own attention to AI debt.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:**
  - CPI 3.72% y/y (75th 10-year percentile), core CPI 2.57% (50th) but running 3.8% annualized over 3 months.
  - PCE 3.65%, core PCE 3.12% (69th).
  - PPI 6.52% (83rd) and still accelerating.
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens fell to 2.23% from 2.46%; the bond market believes the energy shock fades. UMich 1-year expectations are 4.6%.
- **Context:** well above 2019's ~2% but far below the 2022 peak near 9%. Direction: sticky near term, with lower oil the main disinflation channel for 2027.

**Labor:**
- **Data:** payrolls +165K in October (3-month average +137K), unemployment 4.1% (47th), initial claims 200K (5th percentile, near decade lows), continuing claims 1.70M and falling, JOLTS 7.52M.
- **Wages:** 3.1% y/y (3.9% 3-month annualized).
- **ADP:** +86K in October; recent weekly readings are positive ([Newsquawk](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/us-adp-employment-change-weekly-1975k-prev-1675k)).
- **What it means:** the minutes call the labor market "stable" and near maximum employment, so nothing argues for a pause.

**Growth:**
- **Data:** Q3 GDP 3.4%, retail sales +6.8% y/y (October ex-autos +0.6%), durable goods +10% y/y, industrial production +2.2% y/y (but -0.4% m/m in October).
- **Surveys:** ISM manufacturing 55.5 and services 54.5 (early November). Empire State slumped to 7.8 in November; Philly Fed was 20.9 in October (the November print is due Thursday).
- **Housing:** weak. Existing sales are 3.75M (a 10-year low), pending sales hit a record low and NAHB is 25.
- **Sentiment:** UMich 48.1 (1st percentile).
- **Conference Board:** n/a this cycle.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** fed funds 4.00% against core PCE 3.1%, a real rate of about +0.9pp, mildly restrictive. Musalem wants 2–2.5%.
- **Yields:** 2Y 4.80% (92nd percentile), 10Y about 5.07–5.10% (98th).
- **Real yields:** 10Y TIPS 2.89% (98th, near the decade high).
- **Curve:** 10Y−2Y +0.27pp, flattening as hikes are priced; 10Y−3M +0.73pp.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Credit and liquidity:** still loose. The Chicago Fed index is -0.48, the HY spread 3.43pp (84th percentile, but stable) and M2 +5.7% y/y.
- **Divergence:** equity volatility is elevated (VIX 24.6) and crypto and metals are deleveraging, but credit has not cracked: the HY ETF is 76.65, above its 76.41 3-month low.
- **Dollar:** the broad dollar is at the 88th percentile.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00, NY Fed 12-month odds 16.1%, CFNAI 3-month average +0.03, Chauvet-Piger 0.4%.
- **What it means:** none is flashing; housing is the only recession-like sector.

**Valuation:**
- **Buffett indicator:** 248% of GDP (95th percentile), far above the 163% dot-com peak on the same measure.
- **Risk premium:** earnings yields are below the 10Y for SPY (-0.76pp), QQQ (-1.38pp) and SOXX (-1.73pp). This is the core reason good earnings (Nvidia's beat today) are not lifting prices.

**Oil:**
- **Prices:** WTI 82.01 and Brent 88.68 today (EIA spot WTI $89.5 on Nov 17, +53% y/y, 88th percentile); OVX 49.9 (80th).
- **Inventories:** a 3.4M-barrel crude build in the latest week followed a 4.7M build the week before ([Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/crude-inventories-11-6-2/)). The IEA sees a Q4 surplus.
- **Iran:** the Gulf is escalating again (the US struck Qeshm; Iran targeted Kuwait and Bahrain), but the market is fading the headlines.
- **OPEC+:** meets Nov 29.

## Fed path
- **Futures (effective rate 3.88%):** Dec 9 implied 4.175% (+30bp), a 100% hike.
  - Implied averages: 4.33% in February 2027, 4.41% in March, 4.63% in June, 4.655% in July.
  - That is about two more 25bp hikes after December.
- **Other pricing:**
  - Kalshi: 25bp 91% / larger 8%, consistent with the futures.
  - FedWatch: 69% for 50bp of cumulative hikes by end-January (Yahoo Finance citation; we could not open CME directly).
- **Speakers:**
  - Waller: 50bp possible.
  - Musalem: several hikes by spring.
  - Logan: at least two more.
  - Daly: hikes likely. Hammack: gradual.
- **Next:** Jefferson (Thursday 09:25) and Cook (Friday 12:15), a hawkish dissenter.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** a range with a downward bias while the 10Y sits at 5%+. SPY is testing its 200-day (730) for a second day; bear watch shows 1 of 10 signs.
- **Leadership:** equal-weight, small caps and transports are improving, helped by falling oil and long yields.
- **Tech and semis:** most exposed. They combine the most negative risk premium, the Fed's AI-debt focus and the chip-export rules.
- **Financials:** supported by the front end, but hit by health-care and credit-sentiment spillovers today.
- **Energy:** losing its role as the hedge as oil breaks down.
- **Housing:** builders bounce on lower long yields, but activity data are at record lows.
- **Curve, dollar and oil:** the curve keeps flattening; the dollar stays firm; oil at $80–90 unless the Gulf escalation reaches supply.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (~55%), overheat with two or three more hikes:**
  - 25bp on Dec 9, one or two more by mid-2027; the 10Y at 4.8–5.3%; equities range-bound with value, defensives and domestic cyclicals leading.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI/PCE at 0.2–0.3% m/m, claims under 230K, HY under 3.75pp.
- **Upside case (~18%, up from 15%), oil-led disinflation:**
  - WTI holds below $85 toward the high $70s, headline CPI under 3% by spring, the Fed stops after one more hike, and the 10Y falls below 4.6%.
  - *Confirmed by:* breakevens under 2.1%, import and PPI goods inflation rolling over, an Iran de-escalation.
- **Downside case (~27%, down from 30%), stagflation or a valuation unwind:**
  - 50bp in December on hot data, or the term premium takes the 10Y above 5.3%, or AI credit stress spreads.
  - *Confirmed by:* the HY ETF below 76.41, SPY closing below 726.8 and its 200-day turning down, QQQ below 646.65, a weak Anthropic debut.
