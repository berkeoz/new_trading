# Macro outlook — November 17, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is still **overheat**: solid growth (Q3 GDP 3.4%), a firm labor market (unemployment 4.1%) and sticky inflation.
- **Retail sales:** October's report confirms the consumer is still spending in nominal terms. The headline was in line, and ex-autos sales beat.
- **Import prices:** they ran hot at +1.1% m/m.
- **Fed pricing:** futures price a certain Dec 9 hike (implied 4.18%, +30bp) and about 4.69% by mid-2027.
- **Fed speakers:** Musalem now talks of "several" hikes by spring.
- **Stocks:** a 10Y near 5.15% and a negative equity risk premium cap equity multiples. Leadership has rotated from AI hardware to defensives, financials and domestic cyclicals.
- **Dollar and oil:** the dollar is at a 3-month high; oil is headline-driven in an $85–95 range.

## What changed
**October retail sales (released Nov 17, 08:30 ET)** ([BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/economics/2026/11/17/us-shoppers-slowed-spending-in-october-with-high-prices-leading-to-caution/), [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4673619-headline-retail-sales-rise-as-expected-in-october-core-sales-beat-on-the-upside?feed_item_type=news), [Quackonomist](https://quackonomist.substack.com/p/quackonomist-pre-market-17-nov-2026)):
- **Headline:** +0.2% m/m to $748.2B, in line with +0.1–0.2% consensus. September was +0.8%, unrevised.
- **Ex autos:** +0.6% vs +0.4% expected. Ex autos and gas: +0.5%. Ex gas stations: +0.1%.
- **Details:**
  - Online retailers rose 1.2%.
  - Furniture (-2.3%) and clothing (-0.7%) fell, and autos were weak.
  - Year on year, sales are up 6.8% (82nd 10-year percentile; 3-month annualized pace 10.4%).
- **Reading:** nominal spending is firm, but part of it is price, not volume. Home Depot's comparable transactions were -0.5% while the average ticket rose 3.0%.

**Also this morning:**
- **Import prices:** +1.1% m/m vs +0.7% expected; nonfuel +0.8%.
- **Musalem:** said the economy "will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027", with a real policy rate of 2–2.5%, about a point above today's ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/alberto-musalem-fed-interest-rates.html)).
- **Yields:** reversed an early 2–4bp rally. The 10Y is 5.147% (+2.6bp), the 5Y 4.964% (+2.9bp) and the 13-week bill 4.15%, a 3-month high.

**Versus the Nov 13 outlook:**
- **Base case:** strengthens. Spending is firm, the pipeline is hot, and Fed speakers are converging on more than one more hike.
- **50bp in December:** less likely. FedWatch is about 4% (from 9–13% after PPI). Musalem's path is "gradual but several", not one big step.
- **Downside risk:** the AI de-rating risk flagged last time materialised: SOXX -28% in 30 days and QQQ below its 200-day.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:**
  - CPI 3.72% y/y (75th percentile), core CPI 2.57% (50th), but core's 3-month annualized pace is 3.8%.
  - PCE 3.65% and core PCE 3.12% (69th; 3-month annualized pace 2.9%).
  - PPI 6.52% y/y (83rd), still accelerating.
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens fell to 2.25% from 2.46%; the bond market sees the oil shock fading. UMich 1-year expectations were 4.6%.
- **Context:** far above 2019's ~2%, well below the 2022 peak near 9%. Direction: sticky, with goods, import prices and energy the swing factors.

**Labor:**
- **Data:** payrolls +165K in October (3-month average +137K), unemployment 4.1% (47th percentile), initial claims 200K (5th) and continuing claims 1.70M (falling), JOLTS 7.52M.
- **Wages:** 3.1% y/y, but the 3-month annualized pace is 3.9%.
- **ADP:** +86K in October; the four-week average to Oct 24 was +19.75K per week ([Newsquawk](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/us-adp-employment-change-weekly-1975k-prev-1675k)).
- **What it means:** too firm for a pause.

**Growth:**
- **Data:** Q3 GDP 3.4%, retail sales +6.8% y/y, industrial production +2.2% y/y (October due 09:15 today, consensus +0.1% m/m), durable goods +10%.
- **Surveys:**
  - ISM manufacturing 55.5 and services 54.5 (early November, per the last outlook).
  - Empire State slumped to 7.8 in November vs 16.7 expected; Philly Fed 20.9.
- **Weak spots:**
  - Housing: starts 1.22M (15th percentile), existing sales 3.75M (a 10-year low).
  - Sentiment: 48.1 (1st percentile).
- **Conference Board:** n/a this cycle.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** fed funds 4.00% against core PCE 3.1%, a real rate of about +0.9pp, mildly restrictive. Musalem wants 2–2.5%.
- **Yields:** 2Y 4.88% (94th) and 10Y 5.15% (98th).
- **Real yields:** 10Y TIPS 2.91% (98th), near the decade high of 2.93%.
- **Curve:** 10Y−2Y +0.29pp, flattening on the year; 10Y−3M +0.78pp.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Credit and liquidity:** loose. The Chicago Fed index is -0.46, the HY spread 3.23pp (down from 3.44) and M2 +5.7% y/y.
- **Divergence:** credit is calm (HY ETF 76.73, above its 76.41 3-month low) while equity volatility is elevated (VIX 22.9, in backwardation).
- **Dollar:** the broad dollar is at the 88th percentile.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00, NY Fed 12-month odds 16.2% (up from 13.4%), CFNAI 3-month average +0.03, Chauvet-Piger 0.4%.
- **What it means:** none is flashing.

**Valuation:**
- **Buffett indicator:** 251% of GDP (95th percentile), down from 255% but far above the 163% dot-com peak on the same measure.
- **Risk premium:** negative. Earnings yields sit below the 10Y: SPY -0.90pp, QQQ -1.48pp, SOXX -1.80pp. This is the mechanism behind the AI de-rating.

**Oil:**
- **Prices:** WTI $87.1 and Brent $93.8 today (spot WTI $89.6 on Nov 11, +53% y/y, 88th percentile); OVX 50.3 (80th).
- **Iran:** says it struck a US radar in Bahrain and that more tankers hit mines; CENTCOM reports the intercepts.
- **Supply:** the IEA sees a Q4 surplus, and Iraq restarted Kirkuk-Ceyhan exports.
- **Inventories:** EIA showed a 4.7M-barrel crude build in the week to Nov 6 vs a 0.9M draw expected; stocks are 414.4M, 4% below the 5-year average ([Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/crude-inventories-11-6-2/)).
- **Gasoline:** $3.78, the lowest since March.

## Fed path
- **Futures (effective rate 3.88%):** Dec 9 implied 4.18% (+30bp), a 100% hike.
  - Implied averages: 4.415% in March 2027, 4.655% in June, 4.685% in July.
  - That is about two more 25bp hikes after December.
- **FedWatch:** about 95% for 25bp and 4% for 50bp, consistent with the futures ([Quackonomist](https://quackonomist.substack.com/p/quackonomist-pre-market-17-nov-2026)).
- **Speakers:**
  - Musalem: several hikes by spring.
  - Waller: a December hike, with 50bp only on a hot core print ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/fed-governor-waller-sees-december-hike-likely.html)). He speaks on payments today.
  - Hammack: gradual. Williams (14:25 today): further adjustment.
  - Logan: at least two more. Daly: more hikes likely.
- **Next:** the minutes on Nov 18 at 14:00 will show how many members wanted a hike in October.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** the index is range-bound to lower while the 10Y holds above 5%. SPY sits 1.4% above its 200-day with 0 of 10 bear signs, and equal-weight and transports outperform.
- **Tech and semis:** most exposed. They combine the most negative risk premium with Nvidia governance and the chip curbs.
- **Financials:** helped by a higher front end.
- **Energy:** the hedge against Gulf incidents.
- **Small caps and homebuilders:** hostage to the 5Y.
- **Defensives (staples, health care):** lead over 1 and 3 months.
- **Curve:** a flattening bias as hikes are priced.
- **Dollar:** firm.
- **Oil:** $85–95 unless the Gulf escalates.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (~55%), overheat with two or three more hikes:**
  - 25bp on Dec 9, then one or two more by mid-2027. The 10Y is 4.8–5.3%; equities range-bound with value, financials and defensives leading.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI/PCE at 0.2–0.3% m/m, payrolls above 100K, claims under 230K, HY under 3.75pp.
- **Upside case (~15%), oil-led disinflation:**
  - An Iran deal, WTI under $80, headline CPI under 3%. The Fed stops after one more hike and the 10Y falls below 4.6%.
  - *Confirmed by:* a signed deal reopening Hormuz, breakevens under 2.1%, import and PPI goods inflation rolling over.
- **Downside case (~30%), stagflation or a valuation unwind:**
  - Core 0.4%+ or Brent above $110 forces 50bp. Or the term premium takes the 10Y above 5.3% and the AI de-rating spreads to the indexes.
  - *Confirmed by:* the HY ETF below 76.41, SPY below its 200-day (729.58), QQQ below 647.05, a withdrawn or weak Nvidia guide tonight.
