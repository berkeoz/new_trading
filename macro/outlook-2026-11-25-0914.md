# Macro outlook — November 25, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is still **overheat**, and today's data made it firmer.
- **Growth and inflation:** Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3%, and October core PCE rose to 3.3% y/y, the highest since November 2023.
- **Fed pricing:** futures price a certain Dec 9 hike (implied 4.14%) and about 4.5% by mid-2027. FedWatch shows about 60% odds of another hike in January.
- **Markets:**
  - The bond market is treating the equity sell-off as a haven trade: the 10Y is down to 4.84%.
  - Stocks are de-rating, led by AI hardware, on tariffs and AI-margin doubts.
  - The dollar is firm, and oil is at 3-month lows, still the main disinflation hope for 2027.

## What changed
**October personal income and outlays, and Q3 GDP (2nd estimate), released Nov 25 at 08:30 ET:**
- **PCE prices:** +0.3% m/m and 3.8% y/y, matching consensus, up from 3.6% ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/economy/october-pce-report-pce-inflation-index-up-38-line-with-expectations)).
- **Core PCE:** +0.3% m/m (in line) and 3.3% y/y, vs 3.2% expected and 3.1% prior ([Unusual Whales](https://unusualwhales.com/news/october-pce-prices-match-forecast-core-tops-2026-11-25)). The data feed shows 3.26%, running 3.6% annualized over 3 months.
- **Q3 GDP:** revised to 4.3% from 3.4%, vs 3.5% expected.
- **Income and spending:** personal income rose 0.7% (vs 0.4% expected), while spending was softer at +0.3% (vs 0.4%).
- **Also today (data feed):** initial claims 189K (the lowest in 10 years) and durable goods +0.2% m/m.
- **Tuesday:** Conference Board confidence jumped to 103.3 (vs about 92 expected), its highest since January 2025 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-consumer-confidence-surged-in-november-302949447.html)).
- **Market reaction:** the data landed on top of a global chip sell-off and new Section 301 tariff proposals on 14 economies ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural)). Nasdaq 100 futures fell about 3% and S&P futures about 2%. The 2Y fell 6.5bp to about 4.61% and the 10Y 6.8bp to 4.84%, as haven demand outweighed the hot core print ([mlq.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/nasdaq-futures-drop-3-as-the-ai-sell-off-returns-and-pce-inflation-hits-38-nvidia-falls-6-premarket/)).

**Versus the Nov 18 outlook:**
- **Base case:** unchanged; the data confirm more hikes.
- **Downside case:** more likely. The AI de-rating is now global (Kospi -6.2%), and trade policy has re-entered as an inflation and growth shock.
- **Upside case:** still alive through oil, which is at the bottom of its range.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:**
  - CPI 3.72% y/y (75th 10-year percentile); core CPI 2.57% y/y but 3.8% annualized over 3 months.
  - PCE 3.85% (78th), core PCE 3.26% (73rd), and the 12-month trend is still rising (2.72% a year ago).
  - PPI 6.52% (83rd) and accelerating, which is pipeline pressure.
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens fell to 2.11%, so the bond market still expects the energy shock to fade. UMich 1-year expectations are 4.7%.
- **Context:** far from 2019's ~2%, well below the 2022 peak near 7% for PCE. Direction: sticky, and new tariffs add upside risk for goods in 2027.

**Labor:**
- **Data:** payrolls +165K in October (3-month average +137K), unemployment 4.1% (47th percentile), initial claims 189K (the lowest of the decade), continuing claims 1.70M, JOLTS 7.52M.
- **Wages:** 3.1% y/y.
- **ADP:** +86K in October (latest found).
- **What it means:** the labor market is tight enough that the Fed sees no reason to pause.

**Growth:**
- **Data:** Q3 GDP 4.3%, retail sales +6.8% y/y, durable goods +12.7% y/y, industrial production +1.7% y/y.
- **Surveys:** ISM manufacturing 55.5 and services 54.5 (early November, latest found). Regional surveys cooled sharply in November: Empire 7.8, Philly 6.3.
- **Housing:** weak (existing sales 3.75M, a 10-year low; permits 12th percentile).
- **Sentiment:** split. UMich is 48 (1st percentile), but Conference Board confidence is 103.3.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** fed funds 4.00% against core PCE 3.3%, a real rate of about +0.7pp, only mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:** 2Y about 4.6% (89th percentile), 10Y 4.84% (98th percentile on the monthly series; 4.72–5.31 over 3 months).
- **Real yields:** 10Y TIPS 2.79% (98th).
- **Curve:** 10Y−2Y +0.28pp, flat; 10Y−3M +0.55pp.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Credit:** the Chicago Fed index is -0.44 (loose, but tightening for 3 months). The HY spread is 3.63pp (92nd percentile, rising), and the HY ETF is near its 3-month low.
- **Money and the dollar:** M2 +5.9% y/y; the broad dollar is at the 90th percentile.
- **What it means:** credit, not volatility, is the gauge to watch for contagion from AI-related debt, which the Fed's October minutes flagged.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00, NY Fed 12-month odds 16.6% (up from 13.4%), CFNAI 3-month average -0.05, Chauvet-Piger 0.4%.
- **What it means:** none is flashing. The economy is not the problem; valuation and policy are.

**Valuation:**
- **Buffett indicator:** 243% of GDP (95th 10-year percentile), down from 255%, but still far above the 163% dot-com peak on the same measure.
- **Risk premium:** earnings yields are below the 10Y for SPY (-0.46pp), QQQ (-1.03pp) and SOXX (-1.16pp). That leaves little room for disappointment, which is why Dell's record backlog was sold.

**Oil:**
- **Prices:** WTI 82.4 and Brent 89.2 today (EIA spot WTI $89.5 on Nov 17, +53% y/y, 88th percentile); OVX 49 (79th).
- **Inventories:** the last EIA report showed a 3.4M-barrel build vs a 0.5M expected draw ([Newsquawk](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/us-eia-crude-oil-stocks-change-nov13-3380m-vs-exp--05m-prev-4657m)).
- **Iran:** Hormuz mine incidents are not adding a premium.
- **OPEC+:** meets Nov 29 amid disagreement over Iraq's baseline ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/oil-market-midweek-wrap-brent-holds-near-90-despite-iran-tensions-opec-meeting-conoco-phillips-sale-in-focus/cNbH5X8R9lK)).

## Fed path
- **Futures (effective rate 3.88%):** the Dec 9 meeting implies 4.14% (+26bp), a 100% hike.
  - Implied averages: 4.14% in January 2027, 4.34% in March, 4.50% in June.
  - That is roughly two more 25bp hikes after December.
- **FedWatch:** about 97% for 25bp in December and about 60% for another in January (Investing.com via mlq.ai). This is slightly less aggressive than the futures strip for early 2027.
- **Speakers:**
  - Williams: inflation points to tighter policy.
  - Cook: waiting risks falling behind.
  - Waller: has kept 50bp on the table.
  - On the other side, Treasury Secretary Bessent warned about overtightening.
- **Next:** the Beige Book (today at 14:00 ET) is the last broad read before the blackout.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** a correction in progress, with S&P futures about 10% off the high. Long-duration tech and semis are most exposed: they have negative risk premiums, AI-margin doubts, and now tariffs on Taiwan, Korea and Japan.
- **Defensives:** health care and staples lead (each +13pp vs SPY over 3 months), and homebuilders bounce on lower long yields.
- **Financials:** supported by the front end, but credit spreads are widening.
- **Energy:** without a tailwind until oil turns.
- **Small caps:** lagging over 3 months (-4.5pp).
- **Rates, dollar and oil:** the curve stays flat while the Fed hikes into strong growth. The dollar is firm. Oil is at $80–90.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (~50%), overheat with two or three more hikes:**
  - 25bp on Dec 9, one or two more by mid-2027; the 10Y at 4.7–5.2%; equities range-bound to lower, led by defensives and domestic value.
  - *Confirmed by:* core PCE / CPI at 0.2–0.3% m/m, claims under 230K, HY under 3.75pp.
- **Upside case (~17%), oil-led disinflation:**
  - WTI toward the high $70s, headline inflation under 3% by spring, the Fed stops after one more hike, and tariff proposals are softened in hearings.
  - *Confirmed by:* breakevens under 2.1%, a calm OPEC+ outcome with more supply, and tariff carve-outs.
- **Downside case (~33%, up from 27%), valuation unwind / stagflation:**
  - The AI de-rating spreads to credit, tariffs plus hikes squeeze margins, or 50bp in December.
  - *Confirmed by:* the HY ETF below 76.33, SPY closing below 701.18 (a 10% drawdown), QQQ below 607.73 (a 20% drawdown), Chinese retaliation.
