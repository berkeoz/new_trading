# Macro outlook — November 13, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is still **overheat**: solid growth (Q3 GDP 3.4%), a firm labor market (unemployment 4.1%) and elevated inflation.
- **PPI:** October PPI was **a touch softer than feared**. It lowers the odds of a 50bp hike on Dec 9 but does not change the direction.
- **Fed pricing:** futures still price a certain hike (implied 4.17%, +29bp) and about 4.67% by mid-2027.
- **Stocks:** a 10Y above 5.1% and a negative equity risk premium keep index upside capped. Leadership is rotating from AI into financials, defensives and cyclicals.
- **Dollar and oil:** the dollar is at a multi-month high; oil trades in a headline-driven $85–100 range.

## What changed
**October PPI (released Nov 13)** ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ppi_11132026.htm)):
- **Final demand:** +0.3% m/m vs +0.4% expected ([Barron's via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/ppi-much-higher-expected-odds-134943355.html)). September was +1.4% as revised.
- **Annual rate:** 6.5% vs 6.6% expected (6.3% prior), the highest since November 2022.
- **Core:**
  - Ex food and energy: +0.3% m/m and 5.0% y/y, in line ([Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/world/2026/11/13/us-producer-prices-rise-03-percent-in-october-below-forecast)).
  - Ex food, energy and trade: +0.5% for the second month, 4.9% y/y.
- **Details:**
  - Goods +0.5% m/m (10.2% y/y, the most since October 2022).
  - Services +0.2% (fifth straight rise, 5.0% y/y).
  - Energy -0.1% m/m (crude -10.3%) but +37% y/y; food +1.3% m/m.
  - Pipeline pressure is goods- and food-led, while services are moderating.

**Market reaction:**
- Before the release, FedWatch had about 14% on 50bp. Afterwards it was about 9% (Barron's) to 13% ([CNBC](https://cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)), with 25bp at 87–91%.
- Yields steepened rather than fell: the 13-week bill -1.6bp, 5Y flat, 10Y +3.2bp to 5.135% and 30Y +4.1bp to 5.47%. Term premium, after two tailed long-end auctions, is now doing the work, not Fed pricing.
- Stocks popped, then reversed on a chip sell-off; AMD fell about 10% and Broadcom was downgraded ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/wall-st-gives-up-early-gains-as-tech-stocks-slide-on-nvidia-turmoil-5026063)).

**Versus the Nov 12 outlook:** the downside trigger ("PPI pass-through brings 50bp") did **not** fire, so the base case strengthens slightly. A second risk is growing alongside inflation: **term premium plus an AI de-rating**. Equities can weaken even when inflation data cooperates.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:** CPI 3.72% y/y (75th 10-year percentile) and core CPI 2.57% (50th), but core's 3-month annualized pace is 3.8%. PCE is 3.65% and core PCE 3.12% (69th).
- **PPI:** 6.52% y/y (83rd; 3-month annualized 9.0%) and still accelerating on a 12-month view.
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens eased to 2.31% from 2.46%, a sign markets see the oil shock as transitory. UMich 1-year expectations were 4.6%.
- **Context:** well above 2019's ~2% but far below the 2022 peak near 9%. Direction: sticky, with goods and energy the swing factors.

**Labor:**
- **Data:** payrolls +165K in October (3-month average +137K), unemployment 4.1% (47th percentile), initial claims 200K (5th percentile) and continuing claims 1.70M (falling), JOLTS 7.52M.
- **Wages:** 3.1% y/y, but the 3-month annualized pace is 3.9%.
- **Other surveys:** ADP was +86K in early November. Conference Board: n/a this cycle.
- **What it means:** too firm for a Fed pause.

**Growth:**
- **Data:** Q3 GDP 3.4%, retail sales +6.3% y/y (79th), industrial production +2.2%, durable goods +10%.
- **Surveys:** ISM manufacturing 55.5 and services 54.5 (early November), Empire 25.5 (92nd), Philly 20.9.
- **Housing:** the weak spot. Starts 1.22M (15th) and existing sales 3.75M (the 10-year low).
- **Consumer sentiment:** 48.1 (1st percentile).

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** fed funds 4.00% against core PCE 3.1%, a real rate of about +0.9pp, mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:** 2Y 4.73% (91st), 10Y 5.135% today (98th) and 10Y TIPS 2.78% (98th), the highest real yields of the decade outside September's 2.93%.
- **Curve:** 10Y−2Y +0.24pp (flattening over the year), 10Y−3M +0.78pp.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Data:** the Chicago Fed index is -0.46 (loose), the HY spread 3.20pp (down from 3.44 in October) and M2 +5.7% y/y.
- **What it means:** credit is calm (the HY ETF is flat at 76.99 today) even as equity volatility rises (VIX 20.5). The broad dollar index is at the 82nd percentile.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00, NY Fed 12-month odds 16.5%, CFNAI 3-month average +0.03, Chauvet-Piger 0.4%.
- **What it means:** none is flashing.

**Valuation:**
- **Buffett indicator:** 256% of GDP, the 97th percentile and near the high of its 10-year data, versus the 163% dot-com peak on the same measure.
- **Risk premium:** negative. SPY's earnings yield is 0.95pp below the 10Y, QQQ's 1.54pp and SOXX's 1.92pp. The richest areas are the most exposed, which today's chip sell-off shows.

**Oil:**
- **Prices:** WTI $89.2 and Brent $96.7 (spot WTI $89.6 on Nov 11, +53% y/y, 88th percentile). OVX is 51.7 (81st).
- **Iran:** says tankers hit mines in Hormuz today ([Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/middle-east/2026/11/13/iran-says-tanker-carrying-lng-hit-mine-suffered-explosion-in-hormuz)), and Trump says no deal is needed.
- **Inventories:** the last EIA report found (week to Oct 30) showed an 865K-barrel draw vs an expected build ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/eia-weekly-us-crude-oil-inventories-k-vs-877k-expected/)). This week's holiday-shifted report: n/a.
- **Next:** OPEC+ meets Nov 29.

## Fed path
- **Futures (effective rate 3.88%):** Dec 9 implied 4.17% (+29bp), a 100% hike. The implied average is 4.40% in March 2027, 4.64% in June and 4.67% in July: about two more 25bp hikes after December.
- **FedWatch after PPI:** 87–91% for 25bp and 9–13% for 50bp, consistent with the futures.
- **Speakers:**
  - Daly (more hikes likely) and Hammack (gradual, no need for 50bp) on Nov 12. Logan wants at least two more; Waller has floated 50bp.
  - Today: Daly (12:00 ET) and Jefferson (19:00 ET) ([TD calendar](https://economics.td.com/domains/economics.td.com/documents/reports/bottom-line/tables/2026-11-06/upcoming.htm)).
- **What PPI does:** the soft headline supports the "gradual" camp over 50bp.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** the index is range-bound below the 50-day averages while the 10Y holds above 5%. Equal-weight, transports and financials are outperforming cap-weight.
- **Tech and semis:** the most exposed, with a negative risk premium, AI-capex doubts, Nvidia governance and export controls. Weakness is now spreading to AMD, Broadcom and Marvell.
- **Financials:** gain from a higher front end and a steeper long end.
- **Energy:** the hedge against Hormuz incidents.
- **Small caps and homebuilders:** sensitive to the 5Y; flat today.
- **Defensives (staples, health care):** lead over 1 and 3 months.
- **Curve and dollar:** a bear steepening bias at the long end; the dollar is firm at multi-month highs.
- **Oil:** $85–100, driven by headlines.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (~55%), overheat with two or three more hikes:**
  - 25bp on Dec 9, then one or two more by mid-2027. The 10Y is 4.8–5.3%; equities range-bound with value, financials and energy leading.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI and PCE at 0.2–0.3% m/m, payrolls above 100K, claims under 230K, HY under 3.75pp.
- **Upside case (~15%), oil-led disinflation:**
  - An Iran deal, WTI under $80, headline CPI under 3%. The Fed stops after one more hike and the 10Y falls below 4.6%.
  - *Confirmed by:* a signed deal reopening Hormuz, breakevens under 2.1%, PPI goods inflation rolling over.
- **Downside case (~30%), stagflation or a valuation unwind:**
  - Core 0.4%+ or Brent above $110 forces 50bp. Or the term premium takes the 10Y above 5.3% and the AI de-rating spreads to the indexes.
  - *Confirmed by:* the HY ETF below 76.41, SPY below its 200-day (729.29), QQQ below 647, a weak Nvidia report on Nov 17.
