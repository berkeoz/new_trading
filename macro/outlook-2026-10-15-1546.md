# Macro outlook — October 15, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is unchanged from this morning. Inflation is sticky and energy-led, the labor market is close to stalling and the consumer is still spending. September PPI came in at 6.3% y/y (5.3% expected), the hottest since 2022. Yet the market prices slightly *less* tightening than yesterday, because an AI-financing scare is pushing money into Treasuries: the 10Y is 5.09%, down 10bp. Futures in the data now show an October 28 hike at 28% (30% this morning) and December 9 at 90% (94%). By the close stocks had recovered. Flows still favour bonds, defensives and rate-sensitive groups over AI-linked equities. The dollar eased to 102.1, and oil is firm after a large inventory draw.

## What changed
- **PPI (September, released 08:30):**
  - Headline was 6.3% y/y vs 5.3% consensus and 5.4% prior ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/us-september-ppi-6-3-vs-5-3-expected/)). The data feed shows 6.35% y/y (from 5.58%), +1.34% m/m and a 3-month annualized rate of 8.1%, at the 82nd 10-year percentile.
  - Secondary calendars cite core at +0.7% m/m and 5.0% y/y (4.4% expected). This is not confirmed against BLS, so treat it as unconfirmed.
  - The pipeline pressure fits the energy shock: WTI is +56% y/y and ISM prices paid are 74–78.
- **Market reaction across the day:**
  - The PPI surprise was swamped by the AI-credit unwind. The 10Y fell as much as 14bp intraday, to about 5.05% ([SmallCapsDaily](https://smallcapsdaily.com/stock-market-today-dow-surges-500-points-as-treasury-yields-tumble-but-ai-stocks-remain-under-pressure/)).
  - Stocks reversed from early losses: the Dow rose about 1%, the S&P about 0.3–0.4%, and the Nasdaq ended about flat.
  - CoreWeave CDS hit a record, implying about 60% five-year default odds ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-dow-sp-500-rise-nasdaq-trims-losses-as-treasury-yields-sink-153611834.html)).
- **EIA (week to Oct 9):** Crude fell 8.6M barrels to 415.6M, after API's 11.1M draw. Gasoline rose 3.5M and distillates fell 2.5M, 12% below the 5-year average ([Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Crude-Oil/EIA-Says-US-Crude-Inventories-Down-Nearly-9-Million-Barrels.html)). This is a second straight large draw (the prior week was -3.2M vs +1.7M expected).
- **Versus the 09:03 outlook:** This is incremental, not a new regime.
  - The stagflation trigger ("PPI and retail sales stay hot") is confirmed.
  - The credit channel is now the swing factor. The equity rebound came even as CoreWeave debt kept falling, so the credit stress has not resolved.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:** Headline CPI is 3.72% y/y (75th percentile; 3-month annualized 4.6%). Core CPI is 2.57% (50th; 3-month annualized 3.4%). Core PCE is 3.01% (65th). PPI is 6.35% (82nd).
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens are anchored at 2.36% (63rd), and UMich 1-year expectations are 4.0%.
- **Trend:** Inflation is re-accelerating from January's 2.4%. That is well above 2019 (about 1.8%) and far below the 2022 peak (about 9%).

**Labor:**
- **Data:** Payrolls rose +29K in September (3-month average +51K, 12th percentile), and unemployment is 4.2%. Claims are 199K (2nd percentile) and continuing claims 1.71M, falling.
- **Other indicators:** JOLTS is 7.08M. Wages are slowing to 3.0% y/y (21st percentile). ADP September was +90K ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/private-sector-jobs-rose-by-90000-in-september-better-than-expected-adp-reports.html)).
- **What it means:** Low hiring and low firing. Labor is not driving inflation.

**Growth:**
- **Data:**
  - Real GDP rose 2.2% in Q2. Retail sales are +6.3% y/y, and +0.8% m/m in September.
  - ISM manufacturing is 54.5 and ISM services 54.9 ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/ism-non-manufacturing-pmi-54-9-versus-55-2-estimate/)). Empire is 25.5 (92nd percentile), Philly 20.9.
  - Housing is weak: starts 1.28M, existing sales 3.99M (8th percentile).
  - Sentiment is depressed: UMich 51.7 (5th percentile) and Conference Board 81.9. The Conference Board leading index is n/a.
- **What it means:** Activity is solid, and the mood is poor.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** Fed funds is 4.00% (upper) and core PCE is 3.0%, giving a real rate of about +1.0pp. That is mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:** The 2Y is 4.79% (92nd percentile). The 10Y monthly reading is 5.21% (98th, the top of its 10-year range), and live it is 5.089%. The 10Y TIPS real yield is 2.88% (98th).
- **Curve:** positive (10Y−2Y +0.37pp, 10Y−3M +0.91pp). The 10Y is about 1.0pp above the 13-week bill (4.088%).

**Financial conditions:**
- **Data:** The Chicago Fed index is -0.50, still loose (52nd percentile). The HY spread is 3.25pp (70th percentile), up from 2.63 in August.
- **Market signals:** HYG bounced 0.5% today, off its 3-month low. M2 is +5.7% y/y.
- **What it means:** Conditions are tightening at the margin through AI-infrastructure credit.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00. NY Fed 12-month odds 11.9% and falling. CFNAI 3-month average +0.01. Chauvet-Piger 0.62%, rising from a very low base.
- **What it means:** Nothing is flashing.

**Valuation:**
- **Data:** The Buffett indicator is 259% of GDP. That is the 97th 10-year percentile and the top of its range, versus 163% at the dot-com peak.
- **Risk premium:** Equity risk premiums vs the 10Y are negative: SPY -1.01pp, QQQ -1.51pp, SOXX -1.79pp.
- **What it means:** High valuations with 5% yields make AI names fragile to financing news.

**Oil:**
- **Prices:** WTI is $96.2 (+56% y/y, 92nd percentile; +16.7% over 3 months). Brent futures are $105.8; EIA's Brent spot reads $125 (98th). OVX is 48.6 (78th), falling.
- **Inventories:** Two straight large draws point to tight inventories. Two-way headline risk remains: reported Iran strike plans versus talk of easing sanctions.

## Fed path
- **Futures (effective rate 3.88%):**
  - Oct 28: implied 3.95%, with a 28% chance of a hike and 72% of a hold.
  - Dec 9: implied 4.175%, with a 90% chance of a hike.
  - The implied rate reaches 4.33% by March and 4.57% by June 2027. That is roughly 2–3 more hikes.
- **CME FedWatch:** about 27% for October earlier today ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nasdaq-futures-dive-1-as-ai-rout-deepens-on-debt-concerns-4950779)), consistent with the data.
- **Fed speakers:**
  - Williams (voter) said one more hike "may be appropriate" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/john-williams-fed-rate-hikes-2027.html)).
  - Daly sees more hikes with no urgency.
  - Hammack (voter) speaks at 16:30 ET and Chair Warsh at 23:30 ET, the last remarks before the blackout. If either leans into the hot data despite the market stress, October odds would rebound above 40%.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** The pressure is on AI capex financing more than on the discount rate. Semis and neoclouds carry the most de-rating risk. Health care, staples, homebuilders and energy benefit from the rotation.
- **Small caps and homebuilders:** get relief from lower long yields, but face a sticky front end.
- **Financials:** helped by a positive curve, but exposed if credit widens.
- **Curve and dollar:** The curve bull-flattened today. The dollar is off its highs.
- **Oil:** $90–105 with an upside skew from inventories, and inflation-sensitive.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (about 45%), sticky inflation with one or two more hikes:**
  - The Fed hikes in December (October is possible) and holds at 4.25–4.5%. The 10Y stays at 4.9–5.4%.
  - Equities chop with defensive and energy leadership.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI at 0.2–0.3% m/m, HY below 350bp, WTI at $85–100.
- **Upside case (about 15%), soft landing:**
  - An Iran de-escalation takes WTI below $80, core cools, and AI credit stabilises.
  - The 10Y falls below 4.9%.
- **Downside case (about 40%), stagflation plus a credit scare:**
  - Hikes continue while AI credit stress spreads, with HY above 375–400bp. Valuations de-rate, or Brent rises above $115.
  - *Confirmed by:* core PCE at 0.3% or more m/m, claims above 250K, CoreWeave or other AI-infrastructure defaults or failed deals.
