# Macro outlook — October 10, 2026
**Big picture:** The US is in a regime of energy-driven headline inflation over a cooling core, with a labor market that has nearly stalled but has not cracked. The Fed raised rates to 3.75–4.00% in September and has signalled one more hike. Futures price a pause on October 28 (82%) and a hike by December (86%). Long yields near 5.2–5.6% and a firm dollar weigh on equity valuations, especially long-duration tech and housing. Oil, above $90 WTI and tied to Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, is the swing factor for headline inflation.

## What changed
**Data correction (this update):** The data feed recalculated August CPI year-on-year. Headline is now **3.35%** (was shown as 3.71%) and core **2.45%** (was 2.76%). The corrected values match the calendar priors (3.4% headline, 2.4% core) that the first outlook flagged as a discrepancy, so that caveat is resolved.
- **What it changes:** Core inflation is lower and still drifting down. It has fallen from 3.11% a year ago to 2.45%, now at the 46th 10-year percentile; the 3-month annualized pace is 2.0%. Headline peaked at 4.17% in May (not 4.27%) and has eased to about 3.3–3.5% since June.
- **What it does not change:** Core PCE (3.01%), the Fed's target measure, and PPI (5.4% y/y) are unchanged. The Fed's hiking bias rests on those and on energy, so the Fed-path view is unchanged. The September CPI on Wednesday is now judged against a cleaner 2.4% core base: consensus core y/y of 2.5% would be a slight uptick.
- **Net:** a slightly more benign inflation read. It modestly raises the odds of the upside case, but not enough to change the base case.

No new prints have been released since the first outlook (earlier today). The inputs behind it:

  - A 25bp hike to 3.75–4.00%, by a 12-0 vote, the first increase since 2023. The statement said inflation remains elevated.

  - In the press conference, Warsh called the move "removing accommodation" and said he is "not in the forward guidance business" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html)).

  - Voters Jefferson, Williams, Waller and Bowman have all pointed to an October pause.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:**
  - Headline CPI 3.35% y/y in August (71st 10-year percentile), up from 2.4% in January. It peaked at 4.17% in May after the spring oil spike.
  - Core CPI 2.45% (46th percentile), down from 3.11% a year ago and drifting lower since May (2.82%). Its 3-month annualized pace is 2.0%.
  - Core PCE, the Fed's target, is 3.01%, near its 12-month range of 2.7–3.2%. PPI is 5.4% y/y.
- **What it means:** The problem is energy-driven headline inflation, not broad core inflation. That is very different from the 2022 peak, when headline CPI was near 9%.
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens are anchored at 2.34%, but UMich 1-year expectations are 4.0% in the data (August), and the October preliminary reading rose to 4.7%.
- **Data note:** After the correction, the data matches calendar sources (August prior 3.4% headline, 2.4% core). The gap between core CPI (2.45%) and core PCE (3.01%) is unusual, since PCE normally runs below CPI. That makes the PCE measure the Fed watches the stickier one.

**Labor:**
- **Data:**
  - Unemployment is 4.2% (55th percentile), in a 4.1–4.5% band for a year.
  - The 3-month average of payrolls is only +51K, with several negative months over the past year.
  - Initial claims are very low at 197K (2nd percentile).
  - JOLTS openings are 7.08M (36th percentile).
  - Wage growth has slowed to 3.0% y/y (21st percentile), from 3.9% a year ago.
- **What it means:** This is low hiring and low firing. Wages pose no inflation threat.

**Growth:**
- **Data:**
  - Real GDP rose 2.2% annualized in Q2. Retail sales are up 5.4% y/y, helped by prices.
  - Industrial production is up 1.4% y/y. Housing starts are weak at 1.28M (28th percentile).
  - UMich sentiment is 51.7 in the data (5th percentile), and the October preliminary reading fell to 46.3.
- **What it means:** Spending holds up while sentiment is near record lows.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** Fed funds is 4.00% (upper bound), giving a real policy rate of about +1.0pp. That is mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:**
  - The 2Y is 4.75% (92nd percentile). The 10Y is about 5.22–5.24%, at the 98th percentile and the top of its 10-year range; it was about 1.9% in 2019 and peaked near 4.2% at the end of 2022.
  - The 10-year TIPS real yield is 2.87% (98th percentile).
- **Curve:** positive (10Y−2Y +0.44pp, 10Y−3M +0.99pp). It is steepening through the long end, which points to term premium and fiscal and inflation worries, not recession pricing.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Data:** The Chicago Fed index is -0.49 (loose), and the high-yield spread is 3.15pp (57th percentile, up from 2.63pp in August). M2 is growing 5.7% y/y. The broad dollar is 121.4 (78th percentile).
- **What it means:** Despite record-high real yields, credit and conditions remain easy. Warsh has said as much.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** The Sahm rule reads 0.00, and the curve is positive. Claims are low.
- **What it means:** Nothing is flashing. The main risk is the downward drift in payroll revisions.

**Oil:**
- **Data:**
  - WTI spot is $96 (+56% y/y, 92nd percentile). EIA Brent spot is $125 (98th percentile), close to the March 2026 high of $127. Futures in the brief data are lower (WTI 91.85, Brent 104.72).
  - OVX is 48 (78th percentile).
  - The latest EIA weekly report showed a 3.2M-barrel crude draw vs a 1.7M build expected; stocks are 424.1M barrels, 1% above the 5-year average ([Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/crude-inventories-10-2/)).
- **What it means:** Prices hinge on Iran and Hormuz talks ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/oil-falls-as-trump-comments-on-iran-talks-ease-supply-concerns.html)).

## Fed path
- **Futures in the data:**
  - Oct 28: 82% hold, 18% hike (implied 3.925%).
  - Dec 9: 86% hike (implied 4.14%).
  - The implied rate climbs to about 4.58% by June 2027, so the market prices roughly two to three more hikes over the next 9 months.
- **CME FedWatch:** about 21.6% for an October hike as of Oct 7 ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/fed-october-ratehike-odds-slide-below-22-ahead-of-minutes-200689107)). That is consistent with the data.
- **Fed speakers:** Voters lean to "pause in October, hike later." Collins and Musalem had flagged a need for further hikes in late September.
- **This week:** Hammack (Mon), Waller, Barkin and Collins (Tue), Bowman and Barkin (Wed) and Warsh late Thursday or Friday will refine the December odds.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** Equity risk premiums are negative (SPY -1.2pp, QQQ -1.8pp, SOXX -2.2pp vs the 10Y), so earnings growth must carry the market.
  - *Tech and semis:* the most exposed to further yield rises.
  - *Financials:* benefit from a positive curve but are hurt by soft regional banks.
  - *Energy:* has outperformed by about 11pp in 3 months on oil.
  - *Small caps, homebuilders and real estate:* lag sharply at 5%+ yields.
- **Curve:** likely to stay positive; a soft core CPI would mainly help the front end.
- **Dollar:** supported by Fed hikes while other central banks hold.
- **Oil:** two-way, driven by headlines.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (about 50%), energy-driven inflation with a soft labor market:**
  - The Fed pauses in October and hikes once in December, then holds.
  - The 10Y holds 5.0–5.4%. Equities grind sideways to higher on earnings, with defensive rotation.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI at 0.2–0.3% m/m, payrolls of 0–100K and WTI at $85–100.
- **Upside case (about 25%), soft landing with easing inflation:**
  - Oil falls after a Hormuz deal and core cools. December hike odds fade and the 10Y returns below 5%.
  - Rate-sensitive groups and small caps catch up.
  - *Confirmed by:* WTI below $80, core CPI at 0.2% or less for two months, and steady claims.
- **Downside case (about 25%), stagflation scare:**
  - Hot core CPI or an oil spike forces October or back-to-back hikes. The 10Y moves above 5.4%, valuations compress, and high-yield spreads widen.
  - Alternatively, payrolls turn negative while inflation stays high.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI at 0.4% or more, Brent above $115, claims above 250K, or a Sahm reading of 0.3 or more.
