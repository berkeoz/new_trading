# Macro outlook — October 15, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is still sticky, energy-led inflation with a near-stalled labor market and resilient spending. This week's data made it hotter: September retail sales rose 0.8% m/m (consensus 0.3%) and PPI 6.3% y/y (consensus 5.3%), right after a hot CPI. Paradoxically, markets now price *less* Fed tightening than yesterday. An AI-financing scare is driving a flight to Treasuries: the 10Y is 5.07%, down 12bp. Futures show an October hike at 30% (from 52% in yesterday's outlook) and December at 94%. That favours bonds and defensives over AI-linked equities in the near term. The dollar has eased off its high, and oil is softening on risk-off but stays elevated.

## What changed
- **Retail sales (Sep, 08:30 today):** +0.8% m/m vs +0.3% consensus. August was revised down to +1.1% from +1.2% ([ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2296562); [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/newsletter/140122)). The data feed shows +0.77% m/m and +6.3% y/y, up from 5.6%, with a 3-month annualized pace of 6.8%. Part of this is price, with gasoline up 9.1% in CPI, but even in real terms spending is not slowing. Control-group details: n/a.
- **PPI (Sep, 08:30 today):** 6.3% y/y vs 5.3% expected and 5.4% prior ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/us-september-ppi-6-3-vs-5-3-expected/)). Secondary calendars cite headline +1.3% m/m and core +0.7% m/m (5.0% y/y vs 4.4% expected). These were not confirmed against BLS, so treat the core numbers as unconfirmed.
- **Other prints today (data feed):** Claims 199K (consensus 200K). Empire State 25.5 (consensus 12, prior 7.6). Philly Fed 20.9 (consensus 28, prior 37.8).
- **Market reaction:** Muted for rates, because the AI selloff dominates.
  - Yields fell sharply overnight (10Y to 5.07%, 2Y about 4.72%) as the Kospi entered a bear market and CoreWeave/Oracle credit sold off. HY spreads widened to 324bp, the widest since March ([ZeroHedge](http://www.zerohedge.com/markets/futures-slide-ai-unwind-goes-global-kospi-crashes-bear-market-treasuries-soar)).
  - CME FedWatch is about 27% for an October hike ([Reuters/Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nasdaq-futures-dive-1-as-ai-rout-deepens-on-debt-concerns-4950779)).
- **Versus the previous outlook (Oct 14):**
  - The downside "stagflation scare" case named "PPI and retail sales stay hot" as a trigger. That has now happened.
  - A second dynamic has appeared: tighter *financial* conditions via AI credit, which does part of the Fed's job. On balance, risk shifts from "Fed overtightens" toward "growth/credit scare with hot inflation".

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:** CPI is 3.72% y/y (75th 10-year percentile; 3-month annualized 4.6%). Core CPI is 2.57% (50th; 3-month annualized 3.4%, up from 2.0%). Core PCE is 3.01% (65th). PPI is 6.3% y/y, versus the August feed value of 5.41% (78th).
- **Pipeline:** ISM services prices hit 74, the highest since July 2022 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/non-manufacturing-pmi/news/589607)), and ISM manufacturing prices are 77.9.
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens are anchored at 2.36%.
- **Trend:** Inflation is rising from January's 2.4% headline. That is far from the 2022 peak (about 9%) but well above 2019 (about 1.8%).

**Labor:**
- **Data:** Unemployment is 4.2% and September payrolls rose only +29K (3-month average +51K, 12th percentile). Claims are 199K (2nd percentile) and continuing claims 1.71M.
- **Other indicators:** JOLTS is 7.08M (36th). Wages are slowing to 3.0% y/y (21st). ADP September was +90K ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/private-sector-jobs-rose-by-90000-in-september-better-than-expected-adp-reports.html)), and ISM services employment edged up to 50.1.
- **What it means:** Low hiring and low firing, so labor is not the inflation driver.

**Growth:**
- **Data:**
  - Real GDP rose 2.2% in Q2. Retail sales are +6.3% y/y (79th percentile).
  - ISM manufacturing is 54.5 and ISM services 54.9 (down from 55.4, below consensus of about 55.2) ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/ism-non-manufacturing-pmi-54-9-versus-55-2-estimate/)). Empire is 25.5 (92nd), Philly 20.9.
  - Housing is weak: starts 1.28M (28th), existing sales 3.99M (8th).
  - Sentiment is near record lows: UMich 51.7 (5th percentile), Conference Board 81.9.
- **What it means:** Activity is solid, and sentiment is depressed by prices.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** Fed funds is 4.00% (upper), giving a real rate of about +1.0pp. That is mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:** The 2Y is about 4.7–4.8% (92nd percentile). The 10Y monthly feed value is 5.21%, 98th percentile and the top of its 10-year range (about 1.9% in 2019, about 3.9% at the end of 2022); it is 5.07% live. The 10Y TIPS real yield is 2.88% (98th).
- **Curve:** positive (10Y−2Y +0.37pp, 10Y−3M +0.91pp).

**Financial conditions:**

- **What it means:** Conditions are tightening at the margin through AI-related credit (CoreWeave CDS about 1,118bp). Watch this most closely.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00. NY Fed 12-month odds 11.9% and falling. CFNAI 3-month average +0.01. Chauvet-Piger 0.6%.
- **What it means:** Nothing is flashing. The Conference Board leading index: n/a.

**Valuation:**
- **Data:** The Buffett indicator is 259% of GDP (97th percentile, the 10-year high zone), versus 163% at the dot-com peak. Equity risk premiums vs the 10Y are negative: SPY -0.98pp, QQQ -1.49pp, SOXX -1.79pp.
- **What it means:** Valuation is the vulnerability now being tested in AI names.

**Oil:**

- **Inventories:** The last EIA report (week to Oct 2) showed a 3.2M-barrel draw vs a 1.7M build expected ([Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/crude-inventories-10-2/)). API shows an 11.1M-barrel draw this week (vs a 1.9M build expected); the EIA report is due today at 12:00 ET.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** The pressure has shifted from discount-rate risk to AI capex financing risk. Semis and AI infrastructure face the most de-rating risk. Health care, staples and energy are the beneficiaries of rotation.
- **Financials:** helped by a positive curve, but exposed if credit widens.
- **Small caps and homebuilders:** get relief from lower long yields, but face hot data that keeps the front end sticky.
- **Curve:** a modest bull flattening today. It would re-steepen if the Fed signals hikes while growth fears grow.
- **Dollar:** off its highs on haven flows into CHF and EUR.
- **Oil:** range-bound at $90–105 with headline spikes.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (about 45%), sticky inflation, a resilient consumer and one or two more hikes:**
  - The Fed hikes in December, maybe October, then holds near 4.25–4.5%. The 10Y holds 4.9–5.4%.
  - Equities chop sideways with defensive and energy leadership; the AI unwind stays orderly.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI at 0.2–0.3% m/m, HY spreads below 350bp, WTI at $85–100.
- **Upside case (about 15%), soft landing:**
  - An Iran settlement takes WTI below $80, core cools, and AI credit stabilises.
  - Hike pricing fades and the 10Y falls below 4.9%. Breadth broadens.
- **Downside case (about 40%, from 35%), stagflation plus a credit scare:**
  - Hot inflation forces hikes while AI-related credit stress spreads (HY above 400bp).
  - Equities de-rate from extreme valuations, or oil spikes on Iran strikes.
  - *Confirmed by:* core PCE at 0.3% or more m/m, HY above 375bp, Brent above $115, claims above 250K.
