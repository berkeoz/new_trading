# Macro outlook — November 6, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is unchanged, and the hawkish side got stronger. Growth is strong, inflation is sticky and energy-led, and the labor market has re-accelerated rather than stalled.
- **Jobs:** October payrolls +165K vs 59–65K expected, unemployment 4.1%, wages +0.4% m/m. August and September were revised up by 84K.
- **Fed:** a December 9 hike is the base case: about 77% on CME FedWatch and 80% in fed funds futures. Futures imply about 4.3% by mid-2027.
- **For markets:**
  - Equities: valuations leave no room, and AI-capex doubts are an extra drag.
  - Bonds: the 10Y is 4.86%, off its 5.31% peak but stalled.
  - Dollar: firm (DXY 102.3).
  - Oil: Brent fell about 17% this week to the high $90s on Iran deal hopes.

## What changed
- **October jobs report (released Nov 6):** ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/us-jobs-report-october-2026), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-today-live-updates.html))
  - Payrolls rose 165K vs 65K (LSEG) / 59K (Dow Jones), with private +159K.
  - Revisions: August to +186K (from +133K), September to +60K (from +29K).
  - The 3-month average is +137K, up from +51K in the last outlook.
  - Unemployment was 4.1% (vs 4.2% expected and prior). Participation was 61.9%, flat.
  - Average hourly earnings rose +0.4% m/m (vs +0.3%) and 3.1% y/y.
  - Breadth was good: professional services +70K, health care +41.6K, transportation +24.8K, manufacturing +19K (vs +5K expected).
  - One soft spot: part-time for economic reasons rose by 339K to 4.8M.
- **Other data this week:**
  - ADP +86K beat about 67–75K.
  - ISM services was 54.5, with prices at 73.0.
  - Initial claims were 189K, the lowest in 10 years of data.
  - UMich sentiment (prelim, Nov) rose to 51.0 from 48.0, and 1-year inflation expectations fell to 4.3% from 4.7% ([CollisionWeek](https://collisionweek.com/2026/11/06/consumer-sentiment-rises-6-3-three-month-high-preliminary-november-reading/)).
  - The Bank of England hiked 25bp on Nov 5.
- **Market reaction:**
  - The 5Y rose 4.0bp, the 10Y 2.2bp and the 30Y 0.9bp, a bear flattener. DXY rose 0.7% and gold fell 0.8%.
  - December hike odds jumped to about 85% just after the release and settled near 77–78% ([ripr/Business Recorder](https://ripr.trade/news/business-recorder/fed-seen-sticking-to-december-rate-hike-after-jobs-data)).
  - Stocks were mixed. The S&P rose 0.3%: chips rebounded and rate-sensitive groups fell.
- **Versus the Nov 3 outlook:** the "labor stall" argument against a hike is gone. Labor is now firm on every measure: payrolls, claims, JOLTS 7.52M and ADP.
  - The base case (a December hike) is now firmer, and the chance of a second hike in early 2027 is higher. Analysts cite April ([MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/industry-trends/october-jobs-shock-seals-case-for-december-fed-rate-hike/596487)).
  - The upside case narrows to an oil-led disinflation story.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:** headline CPI is 3.72% y/y (75th 10-year percentile; 3-month annualized 4.6%) and core CPI 2.57% (50th). PCE is 3.65%; core PCE 3.12% (69th; 3-month annualized 2.9%). PPI is 6.35% (82nd; 3-month annualized 8.1%).
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens fell to 2.29% (57th) from 2.46%. UMich 1-year expectations fell to 4.3%.
- **Trend:** headline is up from 2.4% in January. It is above 2019's ~2% and far below the 2022 peak near 9%.
- **Next:** October CPI (Tue) has a consensus of +0.3% headline and +0.2% core m/m. The oil drop since mid-October should start to show in November's data.

**Labor:**
- **Data:** payrolls +165K (3-month average +137K), unemployment 4.1% (47th percentile; Sahm 0.00), claims 189K (0th percentile), continuing claims 1.72M (27th), JOLTS 7.52M (62nd).
- **Wages:** 3.1% y/y (23rd), but the 3-month annualized rate is 3.9%.
- **What it means:** a tight, low-firing market whose hiring is picking up again. Wages are firming at the margin, which removes the Fed's reason to stop.

**Growth:**
- **Data:** Q3 GDP 3.4% annualized, retail sales +6.3% y/y (79th), industrial production +2.2%, durable goods +10% y/y.
- **Surveys:** ISM manufacturing 55.5 and services 54.5; Empire 25.5 (92nd) and Philly 20.9.
- **Housing:** weak. Starts 1.22M (15th), existing sales 3.99M (8th).
- **Consumer sentiment:** the 51.0 reading is still in the 1st percentile historically. Consumers cite prices and a possible AI bubble.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** fed funds 4.00% (upper) against core PCE 3.1%, a real rate of about +0.9pp. That is mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:** 2Y 4.46% (Nov 5; 87th), 10Y 4.86% (97th area), 10Y TIPS 2.60% (98th).
- **Curve:** 10Y−2Y +0.35pp and 10Y−3M +0.67pp, normal and slowly flattening.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Data:** the Chicago Fed index is -0.46, loose. The HY spread is 3.48pp (86th), up from 2.63 in August; the high-yield ETF is 77.23 vs its 76.81 3-month low. M2 is +5.7% y/y.
- **What it means:** conditions are loose overall, but credit has softened for three months. Equity volatility is elevated: VIX 25.6, in backwardation.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00, NY Fed 12-month odds 16.5%, CFNAI 3-month average +0.03, Chauvet-Piger 0.4%.
- **What it means:** none is flashing.

**Valuation:**
- **Buffett indicator:** 249% of GDP (95th percentile, down from 255%), still far above the 163% dot-com peak on the same measure.
- **Risk premium:** earnings yields vs the 10Y are negative: SPY -0.58pp, QQQ -1.16pp. This week's 4–6% index drawdowns show how little cushion there is.

**Oil:**
- **Prices:** WTI futures $90.3 (spot $99.6 on Nov 3; +70% y/y, 92nd percentile). Brent futures $97.8. OVX 60 (92nd).
- **Inventories:** the latest EIA report (week to Oct 30) showed a crude draw of 865K barrels vs an expected 877K build. Distillates fell 3.6M barrels and refinery use was 94.5% ([Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Crude-Oil/US-Oil-Inventories-Fall-Further-As-Demand-Holds.html), [BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/04/us-crude-stocks-gasoline-and-distillate-inventories-fall-eia-2/)).
- **Drivers:** the Iran interim deal is unsigned and tanker fires continue. OPEC+ meets Nov 29.

## Fed path
- **Futures (effective rate 3.88%):** Dec 9 implied 4.08%, a hike priced at 80%. The implied average is 4.17% in March, 4.31% in June and 4.32% in July 2027. That is about one more hike after December, pulled slightly earlier.
- **FedWatch:** about 77% (OPB/AP), up from about 67% a week ago. That is consistent with the futures.
- **Signals:** Hammack and Barkin back gradual hikes, and Musalem says inflation is businesses' top concern. Cook and Hammack dissented for a hike in October.
- **Next:** CPI on Nov 10 is the last major print the Fed sees before the meeting except PPI and retail sales. A 0.2% core would not stop December but would cap the cycle.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** strong earnings growth is up against a rising policy rate and negative risk premiums. Expect indexes to chop around their 200-days (SPY 727.65).
- **Tech and semis:** the most exposed. AI-capex doubts and policy (data-center moratoria) add to rate pressure; SOXX is 14% below its 200-day.
- **Financials:** benefit from a higher front end; insurers too.
- **Small caps and homebuilders:** suffer from higher-for-longer. Homebuilders fell 2.1% Friday.
- **Energy:** loses its premium if a deal lands.
- **Defensives:** health care and staples have led for a month.
- **Curve:** a flattening bias as the front end prices hikes.
- **Dollar:** firm on US outperformance (DXY near its 3-month high).
- **Oil:** binary on Iran.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (~55%), sticky inflation with one or two more hikes:**
  - A December hike to 4.00–4.25%, possibly one more by spring. The 10Y is 4.7–5.2%; equities range-bound with defensive leadership.
  - *Confirmed by:* core CPI 0.2–0.3% m/m, payrolls 75–175K, claims under 230K, HY under 3.75pp.
- **Upside case (~15%), oil-led disinflation and a soft landing:**
  - A signed Iran deal pushes WTI below $85 and headline CPI falls toward 3%. The Fed stops after December, and the 10Y drops below 4.6%.
  - *Confirmed by:* a signed deal, breakevens under 2.2%, small caps and transports leading.
- **Downside case (~30%), stagflation plus a valuation or credit scare:**
  - Core at 0.4%+ and a 10Y above 5.3% bring hikes into mid-2027. The AI-capex funding strain spreads to credit.
  - *Confirmed by:* the HY ETF below 76.81 / spread above 3.75pp, Brent above $115, SPY below its 200-day with falling breadth.
