# Macro outlook — October 28, 2026
**Big picture:** The regime is the same as two weeks ago: sticky, energy-led inflation, a labor market that is barely hiring, and a consumer that is still spending.

The Fed made it explicit today. It held at 3.75–4.00% after September's hike, but two officials (Hammack and Cook) voted to hike now. The statement upgraded growth to "strong" and dropped any promise about the path. Futures price a December hike at 90% (FedWatch: 82.6%) and about two more hikes by mid-2027.

For markets, this means:
- **Bonds:** long yields above 5% (10Y 5.045%).
- **Dollar:** firm, near 102.
- **Oil:** above $100, with upside skew from Iran strike plans.
- **Equities:** narrow, with energy and defensives leading over 1–3 months and AI names swinging on financing news.

## What changed
- **FOMC (Oct 28):**
  - **Decision:** hold at 3.75–4.00%, 10–2. Cleveland's Hammack and Governor Cook dissented for a 25bp hike. This is the first pause after September's 25bp increase.
  - **Statement:** activity was upgraded from "solid" to "expanding at a strong pace", and "inflation remains elevated" stays. The line that the September hike would support a "more timely return of inflation to 2 percent" was replaced with "accumulating more information will help the Committee determine the appropriate stance of policy" ([404K Research](https://404kresearch.substack.com/p/fed-holds-rates-steady-as-two-dissent), [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/federal-reserve-interest-rate-decision-october-28-2026)).
  - **Press conference tone: hawkish on goals, agnostic on timing.** Warsh said there is "no soft inflation target and no implicit inflation target." He called financial conditions "tighter, but not tight" and the labor market "quite positive." He said politics played no role, and he does not believe in forward guidance outside a crisis. He flagged the recent rise in oil futures.
  - **Market reaction:** muted for stocks (S&P 500 +0.28%). The curve bear-steepened: the 10Y rose 5.2bp, the 30Y 5.6bp and the 5Y 3.1bp, while the 13-week bill fell 4.5bp. That is the market reading "no hike now, more hikes later." FedWatch December hike odds rose to 82.6% from 78.3% the day before.
- **Other inputs since the Oct 15 outlook:**
  - Conference Board confidence for October was 91.7 vs ~81 expected ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-consumer-confidence-increased-in-october-302920731.html)).
  - September durable goods rose 1.8% (data: +9.9% y/y).
  - The Bank of Canada hiked to 2.50% today, a sign that global tightening is not just a Fed story.
  - The EIA reported a 1.9M-barrel crude draw (week to Oct 23); stocks are 4% below the 5-year average ([Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Crude-Oil/US-Oil-Inventories-Fall-Again-As-Prices-Rise.html)).
- **Versus the Oct 15 outlook:** the base case (one or two more hikes, 10Y 4.9–5.4%) is confirmed. The AI-credit scare has eased: HY spreads are 3.18pp vs 3.25pp. Tonight's Alphabet miss and negative free cash flow show the AI-financing theme is not over.

## Pillars
**Inflation:**
- **Data:** headline CPI is 3.72% y/y (75th 10-year percentile; 3-month annualized 4.6%). Core CPI is 2.57% (3.4% annualized). Core PCE is 3.01% (65th). PPI is 6.35% (82nd; 3-month annualized 8.1%), which points to pipeline pressure.
- **Expectations:** 5-year breakevens rose to 2.49% (78th, from 2.36%). UMich 1-year expectations are 4.6%.
- **Trend:** up from January's 2.4% headline. That is well above 2019's ~1.8% and far below the 2022 peak of ~9%.

**Labor:**
- **Data:** payrolls +29K in September (3-month average +51K, 12th percentile). Unemployment is 4.2%. Claims are 194K (2nd percentile) and continuing claims 1.73M.
- **Openings and wages:** JOLTS 7.08M. Wages are cooling to 3.0% y/y (21st). The latest ADP print in hand is September at +90K ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/private-sector-jobs-rose-by-90000-in-september-better-than-expected-adp-reports.html)).
- **What it means:** low hiring and low firing. Wages are not the inflation source.

**Growth:**
- **Data:**
  - Q2 GDP was 2.2%, and Q3 consensus is 3.2% (Thursday).
  - Retail sales are +6.3% y/y and industrial production +2.2%. Empire (25.5) and Philly (20.9) are expanding.
  - The latest ISM readings (September) are 54.5 for manufacturing and 54.9 for services ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/ism-non-manufacturing-pmi-54-9-versus-55-2-estimate/)).
  - Housing is weak: starts 1.22M (15th percentile), existing sales 3.99M (8th).
  - Sentiment is split: UMich 48.1 (1st percentile) vs Conference Board 91.7.
  - The Conference Board leading index is n/a.
- **What it means:** solid activity with a gloomy mood.

**Fed & rates:**
- **Policy rate:** fed funds is 4.00% (upper) and core PCE is 3.0%, a real rate of about +1.0pp. That is mildly restrictive.
- **Yields:** the 2Y is 4.70% (90th). The 10Y is 5.045% live (98th; its 10-year range tops out at 5.29). The 10Y TIPS real yield is 2.64% (98th).
- **Curve:** 10Y−2Y +0.38pp, 10Y−3M +0.87pp. Normal, and steepening today.

**Financial conditions:**
- **Data:** the Chicago Fed index is -0.49, loose (57th percentile). The HY spread is 3.18pp (62nd), up from 2.63 in August. M2 is +5.7% y/y.
- **Fed's view:** Warsh's "tighter, but not tight" matches the data.

**Recession signals:**
- **Data:** Sahm 0.00; NY Fed 12-month odds 13.3%; CFNAI 3-month average +0.03; Chauvet-Piger 0.62%.
- **What it means:** nothing is flashing.

**Valuation:**
- **Buffett indicator:** 260% of GDP, a new 10-year high (97th percentile). The dot-com peak was 163% on the same measure.
- **Risk premium:** equity risk premiums vs the 10Y are negative: SPY -0.97pp, QQQ -1.45pp, SOXX -1.79pp.
- **What it means:** expensive stocks against 5% bonds leave little room for disappointments like Alphabet's.

**Oil:**
- **Prices:** WTI is $101.9 (+67% y/y, 96th percentile; +22% over 3 months). Brent settled ~$115 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/oil-price-today-wti-brent-hormuz.html)). OVX is 55 (87th).
- **Drivers:** reported US-Israeli plans for strikes on Iran after the midterms ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/us-israel-said-to-draft-plans-for-resumed-strikes-on-iran-after-midterm-elections/)), Houthi attacks on Saudi assets, the Hormuz restrictions, and tight US inventories.

## Fed path
- **Futures (effective rate 3.88%):** Dec 9 implied 4.105%, a hike priced at 90%, hold 10%. The implied average rate reaches 4.255% in March and 4.49% by June 2027, about two to three hikes in total.
- **CME FedWatch:** 82.6% for December ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/federal-reserve-interest-rate-decision-october-28-2026)), close to the data. Yahoo reported ~85% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-rise-as-fed-holds-rates-steady-in-split-vote-185837696.html)).
- **Signals:** two voters already want a hike. Warsh refuses to pre-commit, and the statement is now explicitly data-dependent. Thursday's PCE and the Nov 6 jobs report are the swing inputs.

## What it means for markets
- **Equities:** index-level support from earnings and growth, but leadership is narrow.
- **Rate-sensitive tech and semis:** remain hostage to the 10Y above 5% and to AI-financing news. Alphabet's negative free cash flow vs Microsoft's beat shows the dispersion.
- **Financials:** gain from a steeper curve, but regional banks fell today as yields rose.
- **Energy:** the oil premium supports it.
- **Small caps, homebuilders and transports:** suffer when long yields rise.
- **Curve, dollar and oil:** the curve steepens on a "hold now, hike later" Fed. The dollar is firm and global peers are tightening too. Oil trades $95–115 with upside event risk.

## Outlook and scenarios (next 3–6 months)
- **Base case (~50%), sticky inflation with one or two more hikes:**
  - December hike to 4.00–4.25%, then a hold. The 10Y is 4.9–5.4%; equities chop with energy and defensive leadership.
  - *Confirmed by:* core PCE at 0.2–0.3% m/m, payrolls 0–100K, HY below 3.5pp, WTI $90–105.
- **Upside case (~15%), soft landing:**
  - An Iran deal takes WTI below $85, core inflation cools, the December hike is priced out and the 10Y falls below 4.8%.
  - *Confirmed by:* core PCE at 0.2% or lower, an oil-supply deal, and breadth broadening (IWM and transports leading).
- **Downside case (~35%), stagflation plus a credit or valuation scare:**
  - Strikes on Iran after Nov 3 push Brent above $125; hikes continue into 2027; AI-capex financing strains spread (more negative free cash flow, wider HY spreads).
  - *Confirmed by:* core PCE at 0.3% or more, HY above 3.75pp, claims above 250K, a 10Y above 5.3%.
