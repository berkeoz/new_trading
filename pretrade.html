2 · Ticker

Momentum: RSI, MACD, Stoch RSI, SMI, WaveTrend

Green = bullish (above its signal line and the midline), red = bearish, amber = mixed. x↑ 2 = crossed up 2 bars ago. OB / OS = overbought / oversold (RSI 70/30, Stoch RSI 80/20, SMI ±40, WaveTrend ±53). These five all measure momentum, so treat agreement between them as one signal, not five.

Trend: KAMA and EMAs

Each cell: the average's level, how far price is above (green) or below (red) it, and its slope over the last 3 bars. KAMA = Kaufman adaptive MA (10, 2, 30).

Key support and resistance

Levels are price zones where the market turned before: daily swing highs and lows (last 2 years) and weekly ones (last 6 years), grouped when within half a daily ATR, plus the 52-week and last week's / month's highs and lows. Strength = daily touches + 2 × weekly touches + 1 per reference level; MAJOR = 4 or more. A level counts as broken on a daily close through it, not an intraday poke. Old support that breaks becomes resistance, and the reverse.

    4 · Risk and position size

    The idea: decide first how much you are willing to lose if the trade is wrong, then let the distance to your stop decide how many shares to buy. A wide stop means fewer shares and a tight stop more, so a loss always costs the same.

      Equal-risk sizing across the three

      Same dollar risk on each ticker with a stop 2 daily ATRs away. The more volatile the ticker, the smaller the position for the same risk; that is why SOXX gets far fewer dollars than SPY.
